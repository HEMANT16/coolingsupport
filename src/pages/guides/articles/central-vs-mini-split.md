This is the most common question I get from homeowners: *"Should I just replace my central air, or should I switch to those mini-split things I keep seeing on YouTube?"*

The answer depends entirely on your house. And I mean the physical house — the ductwork, the layout, the number of rooms, the age of the building. Get this decision wrong and you'll waste thousands of dollars on a system that doesn't fit.

Here is the honest breakdown.

## How Central AC Works

A central air conditioning system uses one large outdoor compressor and one indoor air handler (usually sitting on top of your furnace). The air handler blows cold air through a network of sheet metal or flex ducts that snake through your attic, walls, or crawlspace. Every room gets a supply register and a return grille.

It's a simple, proven system. One compressor cools the entire house through one set of ductwork. The problem is that the ductwork is the weakest link.

## How a Ductless Mini-Split Works

A ductless mini-split skips the ductwork entirely. A small outdoor compressor connects directly to one or more wall-mounted indoor units via a thin copper refrigerant line and a drain hose. Each indoor unit cools only the room it's in.

There's no attic, no ducts, no supply registers. Refrigerant goes straight from the outdoor unit to the wall unit. That's it.

## The Efficiency Gap: 75% vs 98% Delivered

Here is the single most important number in this entire comparison.

According to the Department of Energy, the average home loses **20–30% of its cooling capacity** through leaky, uninsulated ductwork. That means for every $1.00 you spend cooling your house with central air, only $0.70–$0.80 actually reaches the rooms. The rest leaks into your attic.

A ductless mini-split has no ducts to leak. Refrigerant travels through insulated copper lines directly to the indoor unit. Delivery efficiency is roughly **98%**.

![Central AC Duct Losses vs Mini-Split Direct Delivery — 75% vs 98% Efficiency](/images/duct_loss_diagram.png)

This is why mini-splits routinely hit 24–33 SEER ratings while central systems top out around 20–22 SEER. It's not just the compressor — it's the delivery system.

## Installation Cost: Where Mini-Splits Get Expensive Fast

A single-zone mini-split (one outdoor unit, one indoor unit) is cheap. Cooling an entire house with mini-splits is brutally expensive. Here is the real-world cost scaling:

| Scenario | Central AC | Mini-Split | Winner |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1 Zone** (garage, addition, bonus room) | $5,500 | **$3,200** | Mini-Split |
| **2 Zones** (master + living room) | $6,500 | $5,800 | Roughly tied |
| **3 Zones** (whole floor) | **$7,500** | $9,800 | Central AC |
| **5 Zones** (whole home, 4BR) | **$8,500** | $16,000 | Central AC |

![Installation Cost Comparison — Central AC vs Mini-Split by Number of Zones](/images/central_vs_mini_cost.png)

Mini-splits win for 1–2 zones. Central AC wins for whole-home cooling. The crossover point is right around 2–3 zones.

The reason is simple. Every additional mini-split zone requires its own indoor head unit ($800–$1,200), its own copper line set, its own condensate drain, and its own electrical connection. The labor adds up fast. A central system uses one set of ductwork for everything.

## Monthly Electric Bill: Zoning Changes Everything

Here is where mini-splits claw back some of that installation cost.

With central AC, you cool the entire house every time the system runs. Sleeping in the master bedroom at night? Too bad — the living room, kitchen, and empty guest room are all getting cooled too. You're paying for 2,000 sq ft of cooling when you only need 300 sq ft.

With mini-splits, you turn on only the zones you're using. Sleeping? Turn on the bedroom unit. Watching TV? Turn on the living room unit. Everything else stays off.

![Zoning Savings — Cooling the Whole Home vs Cooling Only the Rooms You Use](/images/zoning_savings_chart.png)

For a typical 4-bedroom house, zoning can cut monthly cooling costs by **40–55%** compared to running a central system 24/7. Over a year, that's $600–$900 in savings depending on your climate and electricity rate.

> [!TIP]
> If you already have central AC and want zoning without ripping everything out, look into **smart vent damper systems** (like Keen or Flair) that close vents in unused rooms. It's not as efficient as true mini-split zoning, but it's a $500 fix instead of a $16,000 replacement.

## Head-to-Head Spec Comparison

| Feature | Central AC | Ductless Mini-Split |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Peak Efficiency (SEER2)** | 18–22 | 24–33 |
| **Delivery Efficiency** | 70–80% (duct losses) | 98% (direct) |
| **Installation (whole home)** | $7,500–$9,000 | $14,000–$18,000 |
| **Installation (1 zone)** | $5,500 | $3,200 |
| **Zoning** | No (whole-house only) | Yes (per-room) |
| **Air Filtration** | 4" media filter, UV light capable | Small washable filter per unit |
| **Aesthetics** | Hidden (ceiling registers) | Wall-mounted units visible |
| **Noise (indoor)** | 35–45 dB (air handler in closet) | 19–26 dB (whisper quiet) |
| **Maintenance** | 1 filter change, 1 coil cleaning/yr | Filter cleaning per unit, monthly |
| **Lifespan** | 12–15 years | 15–20 years |

## Maintenance: Central AC Is Easier

Central AC maintenance is simple. Change the filter every 3 months. Have a tech clean the outdoor coil once a year. Done.

Mini-splits require more hands-on work. Each indoor head unit has a washable filter that should be cleaned every 2–4 weeks. The blower wheel inside the unit collects mold and dust over time (especially in humid climates) and needs a deep cleaning every 1–2 years. If you have 5 indoor units, that's 5 separate filter cleanings and 5 separate deep cleans.

If you're the type who forgets to change a furnace filter for a year, mini-splits will punish you with mold and reduced airflow within months.

## When to Choose Each System

**Choose Central AC if:**
*   Your home already has good ductwork (sealed, insulated, no major leaks).
*   You want to cool the entire house and don't care about room-by-room zoning.
*   You want better whole-home air filtration (4" MERV-13 filters, UV lights).
*   You hate the look of wall-mounted indoor units.
*   You want simple, low-maintenance operation.

**Choose a Ductless Mini-Split if:**
*   Your home has no ductwork (old house, radiator heat, baseboard heat).
*   You're adding a room (garage conversion, sunroom, bonus room above garage).
*   You want to cool only 1–2 specific rooms that are always too hot.
*   You want maximum energy efficiency and are willing to maintain each unit.
*   Your electricity is expensive ($0.25+/kWh) and zoning savings matter.

**The hybrid play:** A lot of homeowners keep their central system for the main living areas and add a single mini-split to the one room that's always too hot (usually the master bedroom or a room above the garage). This is often the smartest, most cost-effective move.
